This page compiles the secret surfaces MiSide actually routes today: the five
puzzle-indexed paper fragments of Level 13, the novella surface, and the
fourteen character profile photos. Everything below is read from the extracted
client of build 19029065 (v0.93L); where the client proves nothing, this page
says so instead of inventing copy.

## Which secrets are actually routed?

Three families of hidden papers are reachable through routed pages right now:

- **World papers** — six rows: the five `paper_part` fragments plus one
  `novella_surface`. They live under `/lore`, re-spaced ids and all, because
  the client ships no display titles for them.
- **Profile documents** — fourteen rows, one photo page per character, all
  bound to the single `mita-profiles` collectible set.
- **Books** — eight shelf books with their own gallery; they are dressing-layer
  catalogue rows rather than secrets, so this page only points at them.

The 160 `note` rows are deliberately absent from the linked lists below. No
routed kind reaches them yet — widening that vocabulary is a governed contract
change, not an editorial decision — so until then notes appear here only as
plain text mentions, never as links.

## Where do the five paper fragments sit?

All five sit in one scene: Level 13, whose client chapter name reads
"She Just Wants to Sleep". The shipped relink rows bind each fragment to that
container with a hard, modeled edge:

- Paperpart Level 13 0 carries puzzle index 0.
- Paperpart Level 13 1 carries puzzle index 1.
- Paperpart Level 13 2 carries puzzle index 2.
- Paperpart Level 13 3 carries puzzle index 3.
- Paperpart Level 13 4 carries puzzle index 4.

The numbered names are the honest labels — the client serializes these papers
with zero payload fields beyond their MonoBehaviour base, so no title exists
to translate. Their readable text is equally unresolved on every row
(`text_mechanism: unresolved`), which is why this compilation describes
placement and wiring only.

## What about the novella surface?

One further world paper is routed: the novella surface. Its id encodes
"location18", but the pinned relink row binds it to the Level 20 container —
the scene the client names "Reading Books, Destroying Glitches". The id is
historical naming; the relink row is the authority this database follows. Its
page carries the same honest label as every title-less paper:
Novella Location 18.

## How do profile photos work?

Each of the fourteen profile documents records a `FlashTaker` carrier — the
in-game flash camera — and eleven of them carry a pinned scene edge. Two
scenes hold two profiles each:

| Profile | Subject scene | Chapter name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short-haired Mita | level12 | (unnamed) |
| Kind Mita | level17 | Cappie |
| Mita | level17 | Cappie |
| Ghostly Mita | level13 | She Just Wants to Sleep |
| Dummy Mita | level13 | She Just Wants to Sleep |

The remaining placed profiles: Cap-wearing Mita in Level 9 ("The Loop"),
Tiny Mita in Level 10 ("Chibi Mita"), Chibi Mita in Level 11 ("Dummies and
Forgotten Puzzles"), Creepy Mita in Level 14 ("Being Candid"), Sleepy Mita in
Level 19 ("Novels"), and Mila in Level 21 ("Run and Hide!"). Three profiles —
2D, Core, and the true Mita — ship no placement edge yet; their pages render
the honest unplaced state rather than a guess.

Two of those table rows share one client string. The usual Mita's photo and
the true Mita's photo both resolve to the byte-identical name "Mita" in every
locale, so neither auto-links anywhere on this page: a wrong-entity link is
worse than no link. Short-haired Mita keeps her link because her string is
unique.

## Are the books and notes secrets too?

Books are shelf dressing with a gallery: eight volumes whose art exists in all
34 client locales except the two Chinese variants, which ship four covers each
(the Location19 pages are absent there). They are catalogued, not hunted.

The 160 notes stay catalogued-only: their placement containers and event
wiring are measured, their serialized text mechanism is unresolved on every
row, and no routed kind reaches them yet. When a contract change widens that
vocabulary, this compilation grows a notes section — written from evidence,
the same as everything above.
