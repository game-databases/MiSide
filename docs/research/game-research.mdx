## 1. Game structure

Single-player narrative horror built as a **metafictional loop**: the
protagonist plays a mobile tamagotchi-style life-sim called *MiSide*, and on
in-game day 37 gets pulled inside it. The game opens in a chibi 2.5D phone-game
view, then shifts to first-person 3D inside the game world — an apartment that
acts as the metaspace/hub the rest of the game travels out of and back into
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiSide)).

### Chapters — 20 units (0–19)

The community documents **20 chapters**: a prologue plus 19 numbered chapters,
each named like a save slot ([Fandom · Campaign](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Campaign),
cross-checked against the load-point list in patch notes below):

| # | Chapter | # | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Prologue | 10 | Ghost Mita |
| 1 | I'm Inside a Game? | 11 | She Just Wants to Sleep |
| 2 | Together at Last | 12 | Novels |
| 3 | Things Get Weird | 13 | Reading Books, Destroying Glitches |
| 4 | The Basement | 14 | Run and Hide! |
| 5 | Beyond the World | 15 | Old Version |
| 6 | Cappie | 16 | Being Candid |
| 7 | The Loop | 17 | The Real World |
| 8 | Chibi Mita | 18 | Reboot |
| 9 | Dummies and Forgotten Puzzles | 19 | "Leave the Core!" |

Save/load points are named per chapter segment; patch 0.921 renamed several of
them officially — `Cap`→`Cappie`, `Open Dialogue`→`Be Candid`,
`Out of the Core`→`Leave The Core`
([Fandom · MiSide/Versions](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/MiSide/Versions)).
Those load-point names are a stable in-game vocabulary our extraction should
preserve verbatim per build (they are diffable across patches).

### The version metaspace

Inside the fiction, the world is layered into **versions of the game itself**
(known run 0.5–1.9 plus the 0.0 exception), each hosting its own Mita; travel
between them happens through
**Portals** (large machines requiring batteries plus objects that correlate the
versions) and small **devices** (e.g. Player 1's ring)
([Fandom · MiSide (Fictional)](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/MiSide_(Fictional))).
Some versions carry IDX identifiers spoken in dialogue — 1.15 is "IDX 809",
1.11 is "IDX 805" (same source). These are exactly the internal identifiers the
extraction doctrine says never to strip.

### Peaceful Mode

A second mode, unlocked behind the "Conditions Met" ending, is present in the
menu but labeled not yet available. Developer statements collected by the wiki
put it at planned-for-H1-2026 as of the February 2025 FAQ, with confirmed
features: day-night cycle, phone shopping, cooking/pizza making, and new
mini-games with costume unlocks, starring Crazy Mita, plus an exclusive ending
([Fandom · Peaceful Mode](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Peaceful_Mode),
[Fandom · MiSide FAQ](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/MiSide_FAQ)). A
Kickstarter campaign tied to the franchise launched 2025-10-31
([Steam news](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2527500/view/814714767715336202)).
**Extraction consequence:** the shipped client already contains Peaceful Mode
stubs/locked state — under Principle zero this is mandatory capture
(unused/cut content is competitively valuable).

## 2. Characters

### Mita variants — the core cast

The wiki models 12 "main" Mitas plus minor and mention-only variants
([Fandom · Mitas](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Mitas)). Each Mita is bound to
a version of the fictional game
([Fandom · MiSide (Fictional)](#the-version-metaspace)); killing a
Mita reboots her with memories wiped except preprogrammed ones
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiSide)).

Main variants (one-line glosses paraphrase wiki descriptions):

- **Crazy Mita** — the antagonist host of version 1.9; a rejected prototype
  that traps players and converts them into cartridges
  ([Fandom · Crazy Mita](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Crazy_Mita),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiSide)).
- **Kind Mita** — benevolent ally in version 1.9's basement; gives the player a
  ring ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiSide)).
- **Cap-Wearing Mita ("Cappie")** — version 1.75 host, namesake of chapter 6;
  official rename from "Cap" in patch 0.921
  ([Fandom · Mitas](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Mitas)).
- **Tiny Mita** ("Broken Mita" in RU materials) — version 1.3B, chapter 7
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Tiny_Mita)).
- **Chibi Mita** — checkpoint-scale Mita; chapter 8
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Chibi_Mita)).
- **Short-haired Mita** — version 1.5, chapter 1
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Short-haired_Mita)).
- **Ghostly Mita** — version ≈1.11 (IDX 805), chapter 10
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Ghostly_Mita)).
- **Sleepy Mita** — version 1.1, chapter 11
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Sleepy_Mita)).
- **2D Mita** — paper-dimension Mita, chapter 12 Novels
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/2D_Mita)).
- **Mila** — version 1.0F human-presenting resident, chapters 13–14
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Mila)).
- **Creepy Mita** — version 0.5, chapter 15 Old Version
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Creepy_Mita)).
- **Core Mita** — version 0.0 system-layer Mita
  ([Fandom](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Core_Mita)).

Minor / unofficial-name variants: Long-legged Mita, Prankster Mita (hosts the
version-1.5 portal), Lower Half Mita, Giant Mita, Braided-Hair Mita, Railway
Chibi Mita, Dummy Mita (mass-produced blanks; see the wiki's "Formation
Process") ([Fandom · Mitas](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Mitas)).

Mention-only: Cool Mita, Pretty Mita, "Mita who couldn't archive her version"
(Wandering Mita), "a 'me' who just can't stop whining", "a 'me' who loves
horsing around", Flower Mita, Ballerina Mita (same source). **Mitaphone** — a
phone-speaker companion entity that also runs a funicular railway — is modeled
as a page of its own ([Fandom · Mitaphone](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Mitaphone)).

### Human players (NPCs)

The wiki models prior players as individual entities
([Fandom · Players](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Players)): **Player 1**
(ring-bearer), the **Limping Person**, Tiny Mita's friend (a bug-monster form;
confirmed by MakenCat in an interview the wiki cites), a red-shirted player seen
on Crazy Mita's basement console, nine **Cartridge Players 2–10** each with an
epithet (the distrusted one, the greedy one, the cosplayer, the tortured one,
the obsessed one, the escapist, the writer, the core player, the engineer), and
seven color-coded **Chibi Players** in Beyond the World. The protagonist
([Fandom · Protagonist](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Protagonist)) is the
player-surrogate entity.

### Monsters & anomalies

Modeled as their own family
([Fandom · Monsters & Anomalies](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Monsters_%26_Anomalies)):
Bug Monster, Flying Monsters, Giant Monster, Glitched Beast, Glitched Spiders,
Mirror Monster, Mimic, Limping Person, Fog.

### Real-world credits

AIHASTO is a two-person Russian team — MakenCat (programming, animation) and
Umeerai (textures, models); Russian voice acting by the DreamCast studio;
Japanese Mita voice by Kana Hanaiwa
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiSide)). Publishers IndieArk and
Shochiku ([Steam appdetails](https://store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails?appids=2527500)).

## 6. Endings & choice structure

Three documented endings
([Fandom · Campaign §Endings](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Campaign)):

1. **"Is This the end?"** — the default: investigate the wardrobe sounds,
   play the full campaign, leave through the front door; Crazy Mita reveals the
   player was being uploaded into a cartridge the whole time
   ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiSide)).
2. **"Safe of life"** — open the basement safe (code **4970**) and pull your
   own cartridge while the console runs. The safe is reachable in chapter 4 at
   upload-progress 17%, chapter 18 at 98%, and appears empty in chapter 19
   ([Fandom · Campaign](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/Campaign)).
3. **"Conditions Met"** — refuse the wardrobe (choose "Stay" at the end of
   chapter 3) with six prerequisites accumulated earlier:
   - *Global condition:* complete the main story once;
   - "Play a console game" in ch 3;
   - "Give the sauce a try" in ch 2;
   - "Avoid looking in the vent" in ch 2 (negative condition);
   - "Collect the refrigerator magnets" in ch 1;
   - "Refrain from looking in the oven" in ch 0 (negative condition).

   Achieving it activates Peaceful Mode (§1). Same source.

Documented branching points therefore include: the wardrobe/stay decision
(ch 3, also the demo boundary — the demo ends at exactly this choice
([Fandom · MiSide/Versions](https://miside.fandom.com/wiki/MiSide/Versions)));
the safe-opening opportunity windows with numeric upload-progress gating; and
six binary micro-choices flagged across chapters 0–3. The main-plot branch
logic (which Mita helps/harms, reboot events) is linear per
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiSide)'s plot summary.

### Gaps the corpus must settle (community cannot answer)

Exact counts for profiles/secrets/save-points; minigame score formulas beyond
threshold anecdotes; the full choice-flag list (only 6 of presumably many are
documented); Peaceful Mode stub contents; version↔IDX registry completeness;
whether cartridge placements shifted in 0.91–0.93L patches. All are
data-answerable from the client (AGENTS.md rule 8) — they belong to the
dataminer, not the frontend.
